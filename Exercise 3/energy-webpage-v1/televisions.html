Television Energy Consumption

Television energy consumption can vary according to screen size, display technology, brightness settings, picture modes and daily viewing time. This page provides general placeholder information for the website exercise.

TV

Screen Size

Larger televisions may require more power depending on the display technology and model. Comparing energy information before purchase can help consumers make informed choices.

⚙

Picture Settings

Brightness and picture modes can influence electricity consumption. An appropriate viewing setting may reduce unnecessary energy use.

⏻

Standby Power

Some televisions continue to consume a small amount of power while in standby mode. Turning equipment off when it is not needed may reduce unnecessary consumption.

ENERGY-SMART HABITS

Simple Ways to Reduce TV Energy Use

Energy-conscious television use does not necessarily require major lifestyle changes. Small habits can make a difference over time.

✓ Turn the television off when nobody is watching.
✓ Avoid leaving the TV running as background noise.
✓ Use a comfortable brightness rather than maximum brightness.
✓ Compare energy information when choosing a new television.

EXAMPLE

Understanding a TV's Power Rating

Suppose a television has a power rating of 150 W and is used for 5 hours per day. Its estimated daily energy consumption is:

Daily energy = (150 W × 5 hours) ÷ 1000 = 0.75 kWh

This is an example only. Actual consumption depends on the specific television model, settings and usage pattern.
